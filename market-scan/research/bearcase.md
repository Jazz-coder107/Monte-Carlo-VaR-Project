## A. Academic evidence: AI is REDUCING freelance demand on these exact platforms

### Finding 1 — Post-ChatGPT, exposed freelancers got fewer jobs and lower earnings (Upwork-type platform)
- **Claim:** Freelancers in AI-substitutable occupations on a large online labor platform saw employment and earnings fall after ChatGPT/DALL-E/Midjourney launched; top-rated freelancers were NOT protected.
- **Evidence:** "Freelancers in AI-exposed occupations experienced a 2 percent decrease in the number of jobs per month and a 5 percent [5.2%] decrease in monthly earnings after ChatGPT's release... suggestive evidence that top freelancers are disproportionately affected by AI."
- **Source:** Hui, Reshef, Zhou, "The Short-Term Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Employment: Evidence from an Online Labor Market," *Organization Science* (2024). SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4527336 ; journal: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/orsc.2023.18441
- **Origin:** Original peer-reviewed study (platform microdata); also circulated as CESifo WP 10601.
- **Tier:** 1
- **Confidence:** Established as a published finding (figures corroborated in 3+ independent search results); paper text not directly opened (egress block).
- **Bear relevance:** The "AI-exposed" categories (writing, coding, image work) are precisely what an AI-services solo operator sells. The top-freelancer result undercuts the standard rebuttal "only mediocre freelancers lose."

### Finding 2 — Job POSTINGS (demand side) fell ~21% for automation-prone work within 8 months of ChatGPT
- **Claim:** Client demand itself contracted: automation-prone job posts on a leading global freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-task posts within 8 months of ChatGPT's launch.
- **Evidence:** "21% decrease in the number of job posts for automation-prone jobs related to writing and coding... within eight months after the introduction of ChatGPT." Category figures reported from the paper: writing −30.37%, software/web/app development −20.62%, engineering −10.42%; image-generation/graphic-design roles −17% after image-AI releases. Sample: ~1.9–2M job postings, 61 countries, July 2021–July 2023.
- **Source:** Demirci, Hannane, Zhu, "Who Is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms," *Management Science* (2025; earlier CESifo WP 11276, SSRN 4991774). https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420 ; https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2024/working-paper/who-ai-replacing-impact-generative-ai-online-freelancing-platforms ; summary: https://d3.harvard.edu/who-is-ai-replacing/
- **Origin:** Original peer-reviewed study of platform posting data (the study most often cited for "ChatGPT reduced Upwork demand").
- **Tier:** 1
- **Confidence:** Established as a published finding (21% figure corroborated in 4+ independent results; the −30/−21/−10/−17 breakdown in 2 results); not directly opened.
- **Bear relevance:** Coding/dev work — the AI-automation seller's home category — fell ~21%, not just writing. Demand destruction hit before agent tools even matured.

### Finding 3 — Largest study (3M postings): demand for substitutable skills down 20–50% vs counterfactual
- **Claim:** Demand for LLM-substitutable skills collapsed 20–50% relative to trend, sharpest for short-term gigs — though complementary-skill demand rose.
- **Evidence:** "Demand for substitutable skills, such as writing and translation, decreased by 20–50% relative to the counterfactual trend, with the sharpest decline observed for short-term (1–3 week) jobs." 3M+ postings, 116 skill clusters.
- **Source:** "Winners and losers of generative AI: Early Evidence of Shifts in Freelancer Demand," *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization* 235 (2025). https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v235y2025ics0167268124004591.html ; https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/the-winners-and-losers-of-generative-ai-in-the-freelance-job-market/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268124004591
- **Origin:** Peer-reviewed original study (Oxford Internet Institute / Copenhagen / Complexity Science Hub team).
- **Tier:** 1
- **Confidence:** Established as published (corroborated across 3 institutional pages in search results); not directly opened. NOTE: this paper is double-edged — see Surprises.
- **Bear relevance:** Short-term, well-scoped gigs — the classic marketplace entry wedge for a new solo operator — are exactly where demand fell hardest.

### Finding 4 — Adjacent labor-market evidence: entry-level AI-exposed employment shrinking (Stanford/ADP)
- **Claim:** Entry-level workers in AI-exposed occupations are seeing large relative employment declines; the mechanism (hiring collapse for junior, well-scoped work) mirrors marketplace dynamics.
- **Evidence:** "Early-career workers (ages 22–25) in AI-exposed occupations experienced 16% relative employment declines... driven not by mass layoffs but by a collapse in hiring." ADP payroll microdata, 730+ occupations.
- **Source:** Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025; Nov 2025 draft). https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/ ; coverage: https://fortune.com/2026/06/27/what-is-ai-impact-entry-level-jobs-stanford-adp-canaries-brynjolfsson-richardson/
- **Origin:** Original working paper on ADP administrative data.
- **Tier:** 1–2 (prominent working paper, not yet peer-reviewed at time of search)
- **Confidence:** Probable (figures corroborated in 3+ results); indirect relevance — W-2 employment, not marketplaces.

## B. Clients DIY with AI instead of hiring

### Finding 5 — Ramp corporate card data: businesses quietly replaced freelancer spend with AI at ~25–30x cost savings
- **Claim:** Corporate spend on labor marketplaces collapsed ~79% as a share of spend while AI-provider spend rose from zero to ~3%; over half of 2022 freelancer-using businesses stopped entirely.
- **Evidence:** "The share of total spend going to labor marketplaces fell from 0.66% in Q4 2021 to 0.14% in Q3 2025... AI model provider share rose from zero to nearly 3%... More than half of the businesses using freelancers in 2022 have stopped entirely... substituted at a rate of about $1 in reduced freelance spend for $0.03 in AI spend."
- **Source:** Ramp Economics Lab, "Ramp data shows companies are replacing freelancers with AI" (published ~Feb 2026). https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-labor-market-impact-freelancers ; coverage: https://slashdot.org/story/26/02/19/1735218/new-study-tracks-how-businesses-quietly-replaced-freelancers-with-ai-tools
- **Origin:** Ramp's proprietary corporate-card/bill-pay panel (skews to US startups/SMBs — Ramp's customer base; selection bias toward AI-forward firms).
- **Tier:** 2 (proprietary data, named team, methodology-backed; not peer reviewed)
- **Confidence:** Probable (figures corroborated in 4 independent results); not directly opened. Strongest single "clients DIY now" datapoint found.

### Finding 6 — Survey: near-half of SMB owners would pick AI over a hire
- **Claim:** 46% of small-business owners would choose AI software over hiring a person for an equivalent task, up 8 points YoY.
- **Evidence:** "Nearly half (46%) of owners said they would choose AI, an eight-point jump from 38% in 2025."
- **Source:** Business.com 2026 Small Business AI Outlook Report. https://www.business.com/articles/ai-usage-smb-workplace-study/
- **Origin:** Business.com survey (self-reported intent, not behavior; sample details unverified).
- **Tier:** 3
- **Confidence:** Thin-to-probable (single result; intent surveys overstate).

## E. Oversupply and competition degradation

- **Claim:** The supply side is flooded with AI-generated bids; clients set traps for bots; winning attention is the bottleneck, and it's getting worse in exactly the AI categories.
- **Evidence:** "GigRadar's analysis of 133,872 outbound proposals sent between December 2025 and February 2026 [found] somewhere between 80 and 90% of Upwork proposals are now AI-generated. Clients know it. They hate it." Platform-mean proposal reply rate 7.45%; saturated AI/ML categories 5–7%; 3+ AI clichés in a cover letter → 4.17% reply rate; 4+ → 0.00%. ## Surprises — including where the bear case is WEAKER than expected

1. **The academic "demand destruction" studies are double-edged.** The largest one (JEBO 2025, 3M postings) found TOTAL demand did not fall — demand for AI-complementary skills ROSE; only substitutable-skill demand collapsed. An operator selling AI implementation is arguably on the winning side of that split. The headline "-21%" (Demirci et al.) is relative to manual-task jobs, not an absolute marketplace decline over time.
2. **AI work is the one growing segment on Upwork.** AI-related GSV: +50%+ YoY to $300M+ annualized (Q4 2025), +40% (Q1 2026), +22% (Q2 2026) — decelerating but growing while everything else shrinks; Upwork's own 2025 report claims freelancers on AI projects earn 44% more per hour, and demand for top AI skills "more than doubled" (Upwork In-Demand Skills 2026 PR). The platform is contracting AROUND a growing AI niche — the thesis's niche.
3. **Both platforms show rising spend per remaining client** (Upwork GSV/client record $5,230, 8th straight sequential rise; Fiverr spend/buyer $368, +15.6%). The story is bifurcation (low-end evaporating, high-end consolidating), not uniform collapse. Bad for a commodity entrant, arguably fine for a high-skill operator who can reach the surviving buyers.
4. **Hui et al.'s finding that TOP freelancers were disproportionately hit** cuts the other way again — it weakens the "just be in the top decile" defense that bulls use. Genuinely surprising and under-cited.
5. **The strongest pure-bear datapoint was not academic but payments data** (Ramp: >50% of freelancer-using businesses stopped entirely; 79% spend-share decline). If it replicates beyond Ramp's AI-forward customer skew, it is devastating for the "clients will keep hiring humans" assumption.
6. **Platform AI (Uma, Fiverr Go) is currently weak** per user reviews ("generic," "no automation beyond writing") — disintermediation-by-platform-AI is a real strategic direction but not yet an operating reality.
7. **Net assessment of surprise:** The bear case on the MARKETPLACE CHANNEL (Upwork/Fiverr as venue: shrinking buyers, AI-spam bidding wars, rising take rates, platform AI competing for the same work) came out STRONGER than expected — two consecutive years of client decline, both platforms now guiding revenue down, both blaming AI. The bear case on AI SERVICES DEMAND itself came out WEAKER than expected — every dataset (JEBO complementarity, Upwork AI GSV, AI hourly premium) shows AI-implementation demand growing even as commodity freelance work dies. The evidence points less to "don't sell AI services" and more to "the marketplace channel specifically is a deteriorating place to sell them, and the AAA-course version of the business is competing at the dying commodity end."
